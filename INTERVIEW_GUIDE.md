# Customer Discovery Interview Guide
### Project K — Workout Tracker

**Principles (The Mom Test):** Ask about their life, not your idea. Past behavior > future intent. Shut up and listen.

---

## Who to Interview

| Segment | Why |
|---|---|
| Gym-goers who currently use an app | Reveal incumbent pain points |
| Gym-goers who track on paper/notes | Understand workarounds |
| Gym-goers who track nothing | Understand barriers to tracking |
| People who started and quit a tracking app | High-signal drop-off data |
| Personal trainers / coaches | Proxy for power-user needs |

Target: **8–10 interviews minimum** before drawing conclusions.

---

## Setup (say this at the start)

> "I'm researching how people approach their workouts — I'm not selling anything today. There are no right or wrong answers; honest reactions are the most helpful thing you can give me. Feel free to push back or say 'I don't know.' Mind if I take notes?"

---

## Section 1 — Their Exercise Relationship (5 min)

1. How long have you been training seriously?
2. Walk me through what a typical workout week looks like for you right now.
3. How did you get started — did someone show you a program, or did you figure it out yourself?
4. How often does your routine actually match your plan?

---

## Section 2 — Tracking Behavior (10 min)

5. Do you track your workouts in any way? *(notes app, paper, spreadsheet, dedicated app — or nothing)*
6. Show me / describe exactly what that looks like. What do you write down?
7. How long have you been doing it that way?
8. What made you start tracking in the first place?
9. *(If they use an app)* Which app? What do you actually use it for day-to-day?
10. *(If they use an app)* What do you ignore or never open in the app?
11. *(If they track nothing)* Have you ever tried tracking? What happened?
12. After a workout, how soon do you log it — or do you?

---

## Section 3 — Pain Points (10 min)

13. What's the most annoying part of tracking your workouts?
14. Tell me about the last time your tracking system let you down.
15. Have you ever lost track of your progress? What happened?
16. What do you wish you could see or know that you currently can't?
17. Have you ever switched apps or methods? What drove the switch?
18. What does your current system get right that you'd never want to lose?

---

## Section 4 — Goals & Motivation (5 min)

19. What are you actually training for right now?
20. How do you know if a training block is working?
21. Do you follow a structured program, or do you decide in the moment?
22. *(If structured)* Where did the program come from — coach, app, YouTube, built it yourself?
23. Have you ever built your own program? How did that go?

---

## Section 5 — Accountability & Social (5 min)

24. Does anyone else know about or care about your training consistency?
25. Have you ever shared workout data with a coach or trainer?
26. Would you want a coach or someone to see your logs? Why / why not?

---

## Section 6 — Light Product Probe (5 min, only if natural)

> Don't pitch. Ask about behavior, not hypotheticals.

27. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about how you track workouts, what would it be?
28. Have you ever paid for a fitness app? What did you pay for, and was it worth it?
29. What would make you switch away from what you use today?
30. *(If they mention a gap)* "You mentioned X — how often does that actually come up?"

---

## Section 7 — Close (2 min)

31. Is there anything about training or tracking that I didn't ask about that you think is important?
32. Who else do you know who takes their training seriously — would any of them be willing to chat with me?

> "Thanks so much. This was genuinely useful. If I build something that solves any of what you described, would it be okay if I reached back out to show you?"

---

## Watch For

| Red flag | What it means |
|---|---|
| "I would probably use that" | Politeness, not signal — follow up: "Have you ever tried something like that?" |
| "That sounds cool" | Compliment trap — ask: "What would make you actually switch to it?" |
| Vague future tense ("I'd want to…") | Push to past behavior: "Have you ever done that?" |
| Enthusiasm with zero current pain | They don't need it badly enough to pay or change habits |
| Specific story with emotional detail | Strong signal — note it verbatim |

---

## Post-Interview Ritual

1. **Within 30 minutes:** write down verbatim quotes, surprises, and anything that contradicted your assumptions.
2. **After 8–10 interviews:** tally which problems came up unprompted more than once.
3. **Build only what multiple people described as a current, recurring pain** — not what they said they'd want.
